## 4. What went well

**The one number this product is allowed to say now has a proof, and the proof caught itself
lying.** Story 5.3's first draft ran 500 trials and asserted the observed proportion — at which point
the tightest scenario cleared 0.95 by 0.8 of a Monte-Carlo standard error, an observation wholly
consistent with a true coverage of 0.94. **Determinism had been mistaken for evidence.** That is
SM-1's own failure mode, found inside the harness built to prevent it, before any reviewer looked. The
gate now runs 8 000 trials judged on a Wilson lower bound, and the review's statistics lens went on to
verify the estimand independently, reproduce the coverage exactly, show the sampler unbiased and show
the gate has discriminating power. Nothing else in this build is licensed to that standard.

**Checks written for one reason kept catching defects of another.** `estimator-one-run-one-bound`
(written in 5.2 about registers) fired on 5.3's new simulation module and revealed that *the harness
was validating a path the product does not take*. Story 1.8's `test_rekey_covers_every_encrypted_
column` caught 5.8's two new encrypted columns, whose omission would have made every later validation
record **not opened** — silently, flatteringly, three stories after that test was written. The 4.12
action registry reddened 5.1 twice. Story 5.7's tripwires fired on contact in 5.8 and were answered
**by removal, never by weakening** — `a_pending_section_is_not_a_zero`'s "at least one pending" leg
would have failed *on success*, and became a biconditional instead.

**A defence was declined on evidence.** In 5.5 the natural hardening — cross-checking a matter
chain's anchor against its `chain_opened` entry — was dropped because **both skeptics reproduced the
forgery with the anchor intact**: the chain is an unkeyed SHA-256 and the anchor a plaintext column,
so it would have closed nothing while looking like a defence. The *claim* was corrected instead of
the code. Refusing to add a guard is harder than adding one, and it is the right instinct.

**Nothing already written was re-chained, renumbered or moved** (5.5) — a migration that rewrote the
chain values would have produced the exact signature of forgery with our own hands.

**Deviations were named, not dressed up.** Three inline reviews, one dead lens, 25 agents lost to an
outage, one seams lens lost to a usage limit: every one of them is written down in the story it
affected, in the register the reader needs.

**The gate never went yellow.** Nine feature commits, every one with ruff clean, all checks green,
import-linter 3/0, the full suite passing, the offline fitness frame green and the client building.

## 5. What did not go well

**The dominant finding of this epic: a decision recorded and not implemented reads exactly like one
that was.** This is A2's dead-lens shape, one level up — at the level of the architecture — and Epic 5
hit it four times, each time on something load-bearing:

- **AD-43** decided *both* halves of the per-*matter* chain fix before anything was built, and neither
  had been implemented. Until 5.5, every *bâtonnier* export had a hole wherever a sibling matter had
  written and **reported tampering on an untampered record, every time**;
- **`HeadEntry.chain`** was journalled outside the restorable store on every head advance from Story
  1.11 onward, and **read by nobody** until 5.9. Three checks compared sequence numbers whose left and
  right sides both lived inside the store;
- **`audit_chain_head` was outside `_BACKUP_TABLES`** since 1.11 — a restore produced a *tenant*
  permanently unable to write, unrepairably. It **touched every backup the deployment holds today**;
- **the banned-phrasing check was English-only** and green for eleven stories over a product whose
  every user-facing string is French.

None of these was a coding error. Each was a written decision that nothing checked the implementation
of. **The instrument for this does not exist**, and it is the single most valuable thing this
retrospective can commission (→ **B3**).

**A requirement two-thirds implemented reads like one that is finished.** FR-23 has four clauses;
three were built across earlier stories and the fourth — the *worklist* line — was claimed by no story
at all. Three lenses found it in 5.4. Same family as above, on the FR side (→ **B4**).

**The three inline-reviewed stories are the least adversarially-read code in the epic, and two of
them produce the artefacts that leave the building.** 5.7 ships the *matter record* to a
*bâtonnier*; 5.8 ships the claim *a human read this*. The inline pass on 5.7 confirmed **one**
finding. That is not evidence that 5.7 is clean — 5.9's fleet found **nine** confirmed defects in code
whose tests were green and whose author had already hunted the project's recurring defect by hand. The
asymmetry is unresolved (→ **B2**).

**The recurring defect did not shrink; it became nameable.** A comparison whose right-hand side is not
the same thing as its left, always failing toward the flattering answer, appeared in **seven of nine
stories**: a run started over a superseded version (5.1); an ordinal keyed on four proxies instead of
the identity they stood for (5.2); an unfitness denominator counting verdicts-so-far instead of the
sample, declaring a *ranking version* unfit at 1/1 (5.4); a reconciliation baseline taken after an
override, so a chain gone quiet could be erased wholesale and invisibly (5.5); `never_validated` as a
subtraction across two different populations, whose clamped negative would have rendered as a
flattering zero (5.8); `recomputable_from_this_document` computed from the server's database and
printed on the reader's document (5.9); and the fork guard that deferred to a truncation test **reading
a different table** — the recurring defect inside the code written to close the recurring defect (5.9).
The A3 lens found several of them. It should be permanent, not an Epic-5 experiment.

**Infrastructure cost real coverage.** A provider usage limit killed the seams lens and two skeptics
in 5.2; a network outage took 25 agents in 5.5; one lens errored in 5.9. Every loss was declared —
that is A2 working — but declaring a loss is not the same as covering the ground.

**A5 regressed.** The value has gone from *committed to a private repository* to *committed to a
public one*, on a deliberate decision, with rotation still pending.

## 6. 🚨 Significant discovery — the live deployment is not the product this epic built

Checked while preparing this retrospective, against the Railway project `apx-mvp`
(`apx-production.up.railway.app`):

| Fact | Consequence |
|---|---|
| The live deployment dates from **2026-07-23** | It is a snapshot from around Story 1.3 — **roughly 45 stories and 7 migrations behind** `master`. Nothing in Epics 2–5 is running anywhere. |
| Builder is **RAILPACK**, not the Dockerfile | `docker/entrypoint.sh` — which holds the fail-closed pre-flights — **never runs in production**. |
| `APX_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `APX_VOLUME_ENCRYPTED` are **not set** | AD-31's encryption-at-rest gate is not satisfied by the running service. |
| `APX_HEAD_JOURNAL` is **not set**, and the service has **no volume mount** | AD-35's outside witness has nowhere to live. Story 5.9's whole mechanism is inert on this host, and since 5.9 a correctly-built container would **refuse to start** here. |
| Region is **us-west2** | The project's first stated non-negotiable is **EU-only** (`CLAUDE.md`, PRD). |
| `MISTRAL_API_KEY` is in the service environment, values redacted | This part is correct — env only, never in the repo. |

**This is not a regression introduced by Epic 5.** It is a demo instance that was accurate when it was
made and has been silently overtaken by the build — the same shape as §5's dominant finding, one layer
further out: *a deployment that exists reads exactly like a deployment that is current.*

**Impact if not addressed:** any demonstration given on that URL shows a product without triage,
without the *audit record*, without the bound; and the EU-only claim — the first thing a French or
Luxembourg firm will ask about — is false of the only running instance. Whoever redeploys will also
discover that the current container cannot start without a journal path on a volume Railway does not
give it, which is exactly the AD-46/AD-35 deployment work nobody owns (→ §7).

  against the 2.13 timed harness before a firm ever does it.

unowned, and with the live host unable to satisfy AD-35 at all.

## 10. Key takeaways

1. **A decision that was recorded and never implemented is indistinguishable from one that was.** Four
   times in this epic, on AD-43, on the head journal, on the backup table set, on the banned-phrasing
   check — and once more on the deployment itself. It is the same hole as a silent review lens, and it
   needs its own instrument (B3).
2. **The estimator is the only claim in this build with a proof behind it, and building the proof is
   what found the flaw.** 500 trials of determinism looked like evidence. The lesson generalises: *a
   number that always comes out the same is not thereby a reliable estimate of anything.*
3. **The recurring defect is now nameable, and naming it works.** It appeared in seven of nine stories
   and the lens named for it caught several. Keep the lens permanently (B7).
4. **An inline review is a narrower review, and saying so is not the same as fixing it.** Three
   stories — including both artefacts a *bâtonnier* reads — have not met the fleet (B2).
5. **The product's first non-negotiable is false of the only instance running.** EU-only, no
   encryption key, no journal, three weeks stale. Nobody was wrong; nobody was watching (B6).
