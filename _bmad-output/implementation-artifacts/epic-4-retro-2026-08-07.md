## 3. What went well

**The harnesses paid for themselves inside the same epic.** Story 4.12's action registry turned the
build red **three times during 4.10** and **twice during 4.13** — each time because a new route or
`core/app` seam existed but was not declared. That is a check catching the exact class of omission it
was written for, on work that did not exist when it was written. Story 4.7's `ranking_sets_are_views`
and `triage_sets_one_derivation` did the same for 4.8/4.9/4.11.

**Two stories drew zero fire, and both for the same reason.** 4.8 (the line) and 4.7 (the views)
came back with no confirmed findings. Both were stories where the *shape* did the work — a cut stored
by *pièce* identity cannot silently move; a view cannot drift from what defines it — rather than a
procedure that has to be followed correctly. Where reversibility and honesty were made structural,
there was very little for a reviewer to find.

**Refuted-but-addressed became a habit, and it was the right one.** In 4.3, 4.4 and 4.5 the team
implemented refuted findings anyway when the change was cheap and principled. 4.4 is the clearest
case: all three lenses independently converged on one insight the skeptics then refuted — and it was
still worth acting on. **Convergence across independent lenses is a signal in its own right, separate
from the verdict.**

**The gate never went yellow.** Every one of the 13 commits landed with ruff clean, every structural
check green, import-linter 3/0, the full suite passing, and the frontend building. No story shipped
with a known-failing check "to be fixed next time".

## 4. What did not go well

**A recurring defect family: a derived value compared against the wrong referent.** This is the
strongest pattern in the epic and it accounts for four of the ~21 confirmed defects, spread across
three stories that were built weeks apart:

- **4.3** — the conditional commit compared the *recorded* case-theory id against the wrong current
  value, so a withdrawn theory produced a **false stale**;
- **4.13** — a line placed over a *non-latest* ranking version was compared against the **latest**
  version's cut, and read **falsely fresh**;
- **4.13** — the *confidence bound*'s population was compared against nothing at all, because no
  observable watched the table that defines it;
- **4.13** — the worklist compared "is this artefact stale?" without asking "is this artefact still
  the live one?", so the offer never discharged.

Every one is the same mistake in different clothing: **the code held a referent that was *nearly*
right**. The dangerous direction is always the same — the artefact reads *fresh* / *valid* / *done*
when it is not. None of these were caught by the type system or by the structural checks; three of
the four were caught by adversarial review, one by self-audit.

**The surface stories and the meta-test stories drew almost half the total findings.** 4.10 (23) and
4.12 (22) together account for ~42 % of everything raised. In both, the confirmed defects clustered
in the *seams between layers* — a client disarming a server guard (`label_seq: null` read as "no
opinion"), an HTTP boundary accepting an identifier the seam trusted, a check leg failing open on a
routing shape it could not read. The engine stories were comparatively clean; the joins were not.

**Reviewer agents die, and a dead lens looks exactly like a clean one.** 4.12 lost 2 skeptics
mid-run. 4.13 lost the **entire security-isolation lens**, which stalled on all six attempts and
returned nothing. In 4.13 this was noticed and stated in the review section, and the ground was
re-covered by hand — but only because someone thought to look. **Silence from a lens is currently
indistinguishable from "found nothing", and that is a hole in the method itself.**

**No retrospective for Epics 1–3.** Three epics of lessons were never harvested. Some of what this
retro is discovering (the referent family, the seam-between-layers pattern) may well have been
visible earlier and cheaper.

**A secret-shaped string sits in the repo.** Surfaced by the 4.13 close-out scan:
`tests/api/test_log_redaction.py` and `tests/_fixtures/secret_violations/hardcoded_key/settings.py`
(both committed in Story 1.8, `8b35467`) contain a value identical to the live Mistral key, labelled
*"a fake key value, never a real one"*. Not introduced by Epic 4; unresolved.

## 5. 🚨 Significant discovery — Epic 5 needs a planning decision before Story 5.1

**There are two different "discarded sets" in this build, and Epic 5's north-star sentence is
computed over one of them.**

1. **`piece_label WHERE label = 'discard'`** — the Story-2.x *relevance verdict* pile. This is what
   `sample_discards` draws from and what `record_recall_review` computes its population from
   (`store.py:1772`, `1810`).
2. **The Epic-4 *discarded set*** — `derive_triage_sets(order, line, pins).discarded`: a **view**,
   never stored (AD-39, Story 4.7), defined by the ranked order, the position of **the line**, and
   the pins.

Epic 5's FR-22 says the draw is *"over the whole **discarded set** within her RBAC scope"* and that
the run *"records the ranking version, the position of **the line**, the RBAC scope and the explicit
identifier list"*. **Recording the ranking version and the line only makes sense if the population is
#2.** But the machinery that exists today — including the confidence bound that Story 4.13 just made
stale-aware, refusable-at-export and copy-safe — is built on #1.

This was made visible by 4.13's review, whose highest-severity confirmed finding was precisely that
*the bound's actual population was observed by nothing*. The fix added a ninth observable over #1.
**That fix is correct for the artefact that exists and wrong for the artefact Epic 5 will build.**

**Impact if not addressed:** Story 5.1 either silently draws over the wrong population (the north-star
sentence describes a set the lawyer never saw on the triage screen), or discovers the ambiguity
mid-implementation and has to redesign the freezing contract — the single item the work breakdown
already flags as most likely to be underestimated.

**Recommendation:** hold a short Epic 5 planning review **before** creating Story 5.1, deciding
explicitly:
- which population the *sampling run* draws over (expected answer: #2, the Epic-4 view);
- whether the legacy `recall_review` / `sample_discards` pair is **superseded** by Epic 5's sampling
  run, or kept as a distinct, differently-named artefact;
- how 4.13's `discard_population` observable is re-pointed (it currently watches #1), and whether the
  `bound` artefact kind is reused or a new kind is added.

## 7. Epic 5 readiness

**What Epic 4 hands over, verified present:**

- the **near-duplicate family identifier** on every ranked row (`family_id`, `is_representative`) —
  without it the estimator draws 40 members of one family as 40 independent observations and the
  bound is wrong *in the unsafe direction*. Recorded because AD-23 required it, before anything
  needed it;
- the **discarded set as a view** over a named ranking version, a line stored by *pièce* identity,
  and pins — everything FR-22's population freeze must record;
- **the freshness machinery** (4.13): a bound already knows it is stale, refuses to export as
  current, and cannot be copied without its staleness. Epic 5's `invalidated-in-flight` state is a
  new state on top of this, not a second mechanism;
- the **append-only audit record** with a monotonic per-*(tenant, matter)* sequence;
- the **never-hard-delete probe** and the evidential-table partition, which protect any table Epic 5
  adds *by default*.

**What Epic 5 must build that does not exist:** the sampling run and its explicit identifier list;
the `invalidated-in-flight` transition; the hypergeometric estimator and the census crossover; **the
simulation harness** for the FR-23/SM-1 gate (a stated 95 % bound must hold in ≥95 % of runs against
populations of known truth, or the product emits counts only); the chained, detectably-incomplete
audit export.

**Blocking before Story 5.1:** A1 only.

## 8. Key takeaways

1. **Where reversibility and honesty were made structural, reviewers found nothing.** The two
   zero-finding stories were the two where the shape — a view, an identity-named cut — made the
   defect unsayable rather than merely forbidden. That is the design instinct to keep spending.
2. **The recurring defect is a comparison against a nearly-right referent**, and it always fails
   toward *falsely fresh*. It is invisible to types and to the existing checks. It needs a named
   lens (A3).
3. **Defects live in the seams, not the engines.** Two stories at layer boundaries produced 42 % of
   all findings.
4. **A silent reviewer is not a clean bill of health.** Until A2 lands, absence of findings from a
   lens must be treated as unknown, not as safe.
5. **Epic 5's population is not the population the current bound is computed over.** Settle it before
   Story 5.1, not during it.
