# PRD Quality Review — APX MVP, First Increment: Mass-Document Triage

*Reviewed against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Target: `prd.md` (976 lines) + `addendum.md` (175 lines).*

## Overall verdict

This is a PRD with an unusually honest spine — the thesis (*"v1 built what can be shown; v2 builds what can be proven"*) is real, most FRs are written as negations of a named v1 defect, the capability/implementation split against `addendum.md` is held better than almost any PRD of this size, and the scope-honesty apparatus (§5, §18, §19, §20) is genuinely load-bearing rather than ceremonial. What is at risk is the centre: **the increment is called mass-document triage and no functional requirement produces the triage.** FR-16…FR-19 all presuppose a ranked order and a per-*pièce* classification that no FR requires, defines an input for, or makes testable — and FR-1's "exactly three user inputs" actively forecloses the user ever stating what she wants ranked *toward*. Around that hole sit a second-order set of problems that all trace to it: the risk estimator (OQ-4) is undefined, SM-C1 is arithmetically the same constraint as SM-2 rather than a counterbalance, and the metric suite has a degenerate optimum (retain everything) that no metric penalises.

The document is safe to hand to `bmad-architecture` for §4.1, §4.2, §4.3 and §4.7 — those are build-ready. §4.4 and §4.5 are not yet a build contract, and `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` will silently invent the ranking model if it is run before FR-16a/FR-18a exist.

## 1. Decision-readiness — **adequate**

The trade-offs are named with what was given up, which is rare. §6.2's `[NOTE FOR PM]` on the drafting reversal states the loss plainly — *"the depth-forcing property is genuinely lost"* — rather than presenting the reversal as costless. §10 states *"Zero-retention … is a **contract clause, not a technical property**"*, which is the sentence most PRDs in this category would have smoothed. §8's "Accepted risk, stated plainly and not smoothed" concedes that *"no mechanism in this PRD prevents"* the drift toward what is buildable, and R-2's residual is *"High. Nothing in this document fixes this."* §12's refusal to lead with AI Act compliance is a decision, argued, with the reason a reader could disagree with. This dimension is not theatre.

Two things hold it back from *strong*. First, **R-1 accepts an over-capacity scope without proposing a cut**. The residual reads *"High and accepted… No mitigation makes the list smaller"* — yet §6.1's in-scope list is 36 FRs for one non-hands-on CTO plus agents, and the PRD's own central metaphor is a tool that refuses to hand back an undifferentiated list and instead *draws the line*. The PRD does to its reader exactly what §1 says a ranked list without a line does to a lawyer. Second, **the two hardest tensions get an OQ rather than a `[NOTE FOR PM]`**: only two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts exist in 976 lines, both in §6.2, neither at OQ-4 (which R-5 rates *"Medium-high and under-specified"* and which blocks two north-star FRs) or at OQ-10 (availability, where §17 and the stated ambition are in flat contradiction). The callout convention is being spent on safe checkpoints.

### Findings
- **high** No proposed de-scope against an accepted over-capacity scope (§18 R-1, §6.1) — R-1's residual is "High and accepted" and the mitigation is sequencing, not reduction; a decision-maker is left with a 36-FR list and no recommended cut line. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` in §6.1 proposing an explicit minimum-viable subset (the addendum §3.3 spine steps 1–4 plus FR-16/FR-27/FR-28) and naming what falls out if capacity binds.
- **medium** `[NOTE FOR PM]` missing at the two real tensions (§7 SM-2/OQ-5, §17/OQ-10, FR-19/OQ-4) — the convention is used twice, both at deferred-scope checkpoints, never at an unresolved contradiction. *Fix:* add `[NOTE FOR PM]` at FR-19 (estimator unspecified, blocks build) and at §17 (availability ambition vs. capacity).

## 2. Substance over theater — **strong**

Three personas, all under four, and each earns its place except one. The sceptic is the design centre and §2.1 says so — *"Design for him and the other two follow"* — and §4.5 is visibly built for him. The associate drives FR-20's cell-by-cell invariant, and the PRD is explicit that it *"came from a practising associate, not from a design preference"*. §1's Vision could not be swapped into another PRD: the Friday-evening baseline, the *"a list that refuses to decide pushes the work back onto the person paying to avoid it"* framing, and the honest downgrade *"This is a **wedge, not a market**"* are all specific. §9's NFRs are mostly product-specific rather than boilerplate — *"tests are the substitute for the engineers who are not on the team"* is a real constraint with a real consequence.

Two spots are furniture. **The partner persona drives no requirement.** His stated jobs are (a) *"When I am offered a matter whose review cost I cannot bid, I want to be able to price it"* and (b) the insurer/*bâtonnier* answer. (b) is served by FR-26's export. (a) is served by nothing: FR-19 prices *moving the line*, not bidding a matter, and no FR produces a review-effort estimate for an untriaged matter. This matters beyond persona hygiene — the partner's pricing job **is** the revenue thesis (addendum §4: *"matters a firm currently declines or under-prices because the review cost is prohibitive"*; §18 R-7's qualifying question *"Refusez-vous des dossiers aujourd'hui ?"*). And §9's "Accessibility and non-technical usability" bullet is the one piece of NFR theatre: it names no standard, no level, no bound — *"Nothing superfluous; no technical vocabulary"* is an adjective, and the section heading promises accessibility it does not deliver.

### Findings
- **high** The partner's primary job-to-be-done has no FR (§2.1, §4, addendum §4) — "price a matter I cannot bid" carries the revenue story and nothing in §4 realizes it; FR-19 prices a line move, not a matter. *Fix:* either add an FR producing a per-*matter* review-effort estimate from the ranking, or move the partner's pricing job to §5 as an explicit non-goal for this increment and state that the revenue story therefore rests on the next one.
- **medium** NFR theatre in §9 "Accessibility and non-technical usability" — adjectives, no bounds, and no accessibility standard named under a heading that promises one. *Fix:* name a target (e.g. keyboard-reachability of every *worklist* action, a WCAG level) or rename the bullet to "Non-technical usability" and drop the accessibility claim.

## 3. Strategic coherence — **adequate**

The thesis is stated, singular, and the features follow it. Every §4 subsection traces to *provable rather than showable*: the *failure register* exists so an absence claim can be honest, *truth status* exists so a guess can never wear a proof's costume, the *content-free projection* exists so "only code travels" is a test rather than a sentence. Prioritisation follows the thesis, not ease — addendum §3.3 sequences payload schema first *because it is irreversible*, and puts the audit surface last *because it consumes all of them*. The MVP scope kind is coherently problem-solving. This is a PRD with an argument, not a backlog with headings.

The weakness is in the metrics, and it is structural. **SM-C1 is not a counter-metric to SM-2 — it is SM-2 restated.** SM-2 measures recall at **the line** against the *gold set*; SM-C1 counts *gold set* relevant items falling in the *discarded set*. For a fixed gold set these are complements: `relevant-in-discarded = total-relevant − relevant-retained`, so "SM-C1 may never rise" and "recall may never fall" are the same constraint expressed in two units. The PRD claims it *"Counterbalances SM-2 and any temptation to make triage look better by retaining less"* — but SM-2 already penalises retaining less, exactly and by construction. The metric that would genuinely counterbalance SM-2 is the one that is absent: **nothing in §7 measures the size of the *retained set* or the reading burden it imposes.** Consequently the whole suite has a degenerate optimum: place **the line** at the very bottom, and SM-2 hits 100% recall, SM-C1 hits zero, SM-8 is satisfied (the line was placed with a stated basis), SM-3/4/5/6/7 are unaffected, and the product has done nothing. UJ-4's edge case explicitly permits this position and instructs the tool not to object. SM-C4 (time to first useful screen) is the only pressure in the opposite direction and it measures latency, not selectivity.

SM-C2 and SM-C3 are, by contrast, genuine and sharp — SM-C2's *"A rise in either means the audit surface has become noise the user has learned to dismiss"* is the precise failure mode of the whole §4.5 investment, and SM-C3 correctly names the *confidence bound* as theatre if the ritual is too expensive to complete. Both are, as OQ-9 concedes, unobservable without telemetry.

### Findings
- **high** SM-C1 is arithmetically equivalent to SM-2, not a counterbalance to it (§7 Counter-metrics) — for a fixed *gold set*, "relevant items below the line may never rise" and "recall at the line may never fall" are the same assertion. *Fix:* replace SM-C1's stated counterbalance role, or keep it and add the metric that actually opposes recall — retained-set size / estimated reading hours per *matter* at the system-placed line.
- **high** The metric suite has a degenerate optimum that no metric penalises (§7, FR-17, UJ-4 edge case) — retaining everything satisfies SM-2, SM-C1 and SM-8 simultaneously while delivering no triage. *Fix:* add a primary or counter-metric bounding the *retained set* as a proportion of the *matter* at the system-placed line, with the explicit rule that it may only be traded against SM-2, never against SM-C1.
- **medium** SM-C4 measures a capability no FR provides (§7, OQ-7) — "time to first useful screen" requires triage over a partially-ingested *corpus*, which A-17/OQ-7 concede is unspecified. An SM with no FR behind it cannot fail a build. *Fix:* either add the partial-triage FR (and state its interaction with the inventory guarantee) or restate SM-C4 as an open design constraint in §19 rather than a counter-metric.

## 4. Done-ness clarity — **thin**

Most of §4 is exemplary on this dimension and should be said so first. FR-4, FR-5, FR-6, FR-8, FR-9, FR-10, FR-14, FR-29, FR-31, FR-32, FR-34 state consequences an engineer could turn into a failing test tomorrow: *"Importing folder A then folder B, where B contains a copy of a file in A, produces one pièce with two recorded provenance paths"*, *"given a corpus seeded with known unique tokens, no output of the primitive contains any of them"*, *"a missing translation is detected at build time and fails the build"*. FR-6's inventory guarantee — `submitted = indexed + failure register entries`, *"at all times, with no third bucket"* — is the single best-specified requirement in the document.

The failure is concentrated and it is at the centre.

**There is no FR that produces the ranking or the classification.** §4.4 opens *"Underneath there is one ranked order and nothing else"* and every FR in it presupposes that order exists. FR-16 says the system *holds* one ranked order per *matter* per ranking version. FR-17 says *"After ranking, **the line** has a position"*. FR-18 says *"Every pièce in the ranking carries a confidence value"*. Nothing states that the system ranks, what the ranking is relative to, or what determines relevance. Relevant **to what**? UJ-1 is explicit that Éléonore selects a folder, names the *matter*, confirms the *RBAC scope*, and walks away — and FR-1 hardens this into a requirement: *"Starting an import job requires exactly three user inputs… No further mandatory configuration screen exists on this path."* So FR-1 forecloses the only place the user could have stated a case theory, an issue, a date range or a query, and §4.4 then assumes a relevance ordering exists. These two FRs contradict each other, and the contradiction sits under the increment's name.

The same hole swallows labelling. The Glossary defines *Triage* as *"reversible ranking **and labelling**"*. UJ-3's whole path is Marc opening *"the cell holding its classification"* and changing it. FR-30 lists *"the triage taxonomy and its labels"* as *configuration-as-data*. OQ-16 asks whether v1's nine-label taxonomy carries over. **No FR requires the system to assign a label to a *pièce*.** FR-20 makes cells editable without saying what is in them. A story-creation workflow reading §4.4 will invent both the ranking model and the label set.

Second cluster: **the risk numbers are unbuildable and the PRD knows it.** FR-19's consequence *"The stated risk figures are derived by a documented method that is reproducible from the audit record"* is a requirement on a method that OQ-4 says does not exist — *"not the statistical method, not whether sampling is simple random or stratified by rank, and not how a risk figure is projected for a candidate position of the line before any sample has been taken there."* FR-22's *"requests a target confidence bound and is given the sample size that achieves it"* and FR-23's `X%` depend on the same missing estimator. The PRD is admirably loud about this (A-12, OQ-4, R-5, addendum §6), but the honesty does not make FR-19 and FR-23 buildable — and they are the north star (SM-1).

Third cluster: **several FRs assert undecidable negatives and call them tests.** FR-9: *"a test asserts that the code contains no alternative embedding path reachable by exception handling or by configuration"*. FR-10: *"No code path performs a bulk deletion… Asserted by test."* FR-14: *"A test asserts that no retrieval code path filters results after they are returned."* FR-30: *"A test asserts that no tenant-specific identifier, name or behaviour appears anywhere in source code."* FR-33: *"No code path substitutes stored, hand-authored or generated content… Asserted by test."* FR-21: *"a full sweep of user-reachable actions produces no reduction in the count"*. As written, these are universal quantifications over program behaviour, which a runtime test cannot establish. The addendum §3.1 knows the right answer — *"grep-able, CI-enforceable properties"* — but that framing never reaches the PRD body, so downstream stories will be written as runtime tests that assert nothing.

Fourth: **"explicit validation act" is load-bearing and undefined.** FR-24's last consequence — *"A value the user never touched is recorded as accepted only if she performed an explicit validation act over it — not by default and not by the passage of time"* — and §13's question 4 both hinge on it. No FR creates the validation act, no glossary entry defines it, and no UJ shows one being performed. The mechanism that distinguishes "a human looked at it" from "a human did not" is the product's core evidentiary claim and it is a phrase.

Fifth, smaller but concrete: FR-2's scale consequence is *"completes without unbounded memory growth"* with A-8 conceding no wall-clock target, and FR-13's is *"Exhaustive search performance is stated against the design target"* with A-11 conceding no latency target. Both are restatements, not requirements — FR-13's in particular says only that a number will be stated somewhere, which no test can fail.

### Findings
- **critical** No FR produces the ranking, and FR-1 forecloses the ranking's input (§4.4 FR-16…FR-19 vs. FR-1) — every triage FR presupposes a relevance ordering; nothing states the system ranks, relative to what, or on what user-supplied criterion, while FR-1 requires "exactly three user inputs" and "no further mandatory configuration screen". *Fix:* add an FR before FR-16 stating that the system produces a ranked order over a *matter*'s *pièces*, naming its inputs explicitly (whether relevance is derived from *matter* metadata alone, from a user-stated issue/case theory, or from a taxonomy), and reconcile it with FR-1 — either the criterion is optional-with-a-default or FR-1's "three inputs" applies to *ingestion* only, not to triage.
- **critical** No FR requires classification/labelling, though the Glossary, UJ-3, FR-30 and OQ-16 all depend on it (§3 *Triage*, §2.3 UJ-3, FR-30, §4.4) — the taxonomy is configurable, its cell is editable, its rightness is an open question, and nothing requires it to be applied. *Fix:* add an FR for per-*pièce* labelling against the *tenant*'s triage taxonomy, with testable consequences (every *pièce* in a ranking carries exactly one label from the configured taxonomy or an explicit "unlabelled" value; a label change is a *change log* entry per FR-20).
- **high** FR-19 and FR-23 are unbuildable pending OQ-4 (§4.4, §4.5) — the estimator behind both the priced statement and the *confidence bound* does not exist, and SM-1 (north star) depends on FR-23. Honest, but a build contract that cannot be built. *Fix:* mark FR-19 and FR-23 as blocked-on-OQ-4 in the FR text itself, and state what the increment ships if no statistician's answer arrives (e.g. the sampling ritual and the *audit record* without the bound, with the sentence reporting counts only).
- **high** "Asserted by test" applied to undecidable universal negatives (FR-9, FR-10, FR-14, FR-21, FR-30, FR-33) — no runtime test can establish "no code path exists"; the addendum's "grep-able, CI-enforceable" framing never reaches the PRD body. *Fix:* restate each as the check it actually is — a static/lint/CI-grep rule, an architecture fitness function, or a bounded runtime probe over an enumerated action set — so story creation writes the right kind of test.
- **high** "Explicit validation act" is undefined and unrequired (FR-24, §13 item 4) — the distinction between "modified" and "accepted as-is" is the product's core evidentiary claim and rests on a phrase no FR creates. *Fix:* add the validation act to §3 and give it an FR (what gesture constitutes it, at what granularity — *pièce*, cell, or page of the triage table — and what *audit record* entry it produces).
- **medium** FR-2 and FR-13 scale consequences are restatements, not requirements (FR-2 last bullet, FR-13 last bullet, §9 Scale) — "without unbounded memory growth" has no bound and "performance is stated" has no threshold; A-8, A-11 and OQ-6 concede this. *Fix:* set provisional targets to be revised from the first measured baseline (as SM-2/OQ-5 already does for recall), or delete the bullets and let §9 + OQ-6 carry the gap honestly rather than dressing it as a consequence.
- **medium** FR-17's "a ranking whose basis is insufficient to place the line" has no criterion (FR-17 third consequence) — "insufficient basis" is untestable without the ranking model of the critical finding above. *Fix:* define the refusal condition once the ranking FR exists.

## 5. Scope honesty — **strong**

The best dimension in the document, and the one that would carry it through a hostile read. §5 does real work rather than listing obvious exclusions: it names *"The fixture layer is deleted, not disabled"* and explains why it is listed — *"deleting it will feel like a loss during the first demo"* — which is a de-scope proposed rather than done silently. §6.2 names the deepest, most emotionally costly cut (Syllogisme drafting) and attaches a `[NOTE FOR PM]` conceding *"the depth-forcing property is genuinely lost"*. §11's Retention bullet and FR-21's A-13 name the never-hard-delete vs. lawful-erasure contradiction and say outright *"this PRD does not resolve the contradiction, it names it."* §18's residual column contains "High and accepted", "Nothing in this document fixes this", "Deferred, not mitigated" — a risk table that refuses to end every row in green is rare enough to be worth citing.

The Assumptions Index round-trips cleanly: 23 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags map onto 22 index entries (A-6 and A-13 each covering two sites), with one index entry (A-2, persona names) having no inline tag — §2.3's note is italic prose, not a tagged assumption.

Two gaps. **Open-items density is at the blocker threshold for a document that calls itself a build contract.** §0 states *"This PRD is the build contract for the first increment"*; §19 holds 17 open questions and §20 holds 22 assumptions. Most are properly commercial or genuinely deferred, but OQ-4 blocks FR-19 and FR-23, OQ-5 leaves SM-2 without an absolute target, OQ-6 leaves §9's entire scale NFR without a number, and OQ-7 leaves SM-C4 without a mechanism. That is four open questions sitting directly under the north star. The rubric's test — high open-item counts on a green-light-to-build PRD is a blocker — is met for §4.4 and §4.5, though not for §4.1–§4.3 and §4.7.

**Zero `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` callouts appear in §4**, where the rubric expects them at points a reader could silently assume coverage. Two concrete places this bites: FR-3's supported-format list names `.msg`, PDF, `.docx`, `.xlsx` and images, and says nothing either way about container formats — yet UJ-1's climax shows a *worklist* line *"1 dossier compressé n'a pas pu être ouvert"* and FR-5 enumerates error class `archive-unopenable`, which implies archives are encountered and fail, without any FR stating whether *ingestion* expands them. Mailbox containers (`.pst`, `.ost`) are unmentioned, in a product whose motivating scenario is *"four years of a matter — roughly 1 700 pièces, mostly `.msg`"*, which in practice arrives as an export. Either archives/mailbox containers are expanded (needs an FR-3 consequence and interacts with FR-4's identity function) or they are not (needs a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]`), and the PRD says neither.

### Findings
- **medium** Container and mailbox formats are neither in scope nor declared out (FR-3, FR-5, UJ-1) — `archive-unopenable` exists as an error class and a compressed folder appears in UJ-1's worklist, but no FR says whether *ingestion* expands `.zip`/`.pst`; the motivating scenario makes this near-certain to arrive. *Fix:* add an FR-3 consequence for container expansion (with its provenance and identity implications for FR-4) or a `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` stating archives enter the *failure register* unexpanded.
- **medium** Four open questions sit under the north star of a document that calls itself a build contract (§0 vs. §19 OQ-4/5/6/7) — the density is fine for §4.1–§4.3 and §4.7 and blocking for §4.4–§4.5. *Fix:* state per-section build-readiness in §0 explicitly, so downstream workflows know which features are contracted and which are still specification.
- **low** A-2 has no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag (§2.3 line 71, §20) — index entry without an inline site; every other entry round-trips. *Fix:* tag the persona-composite note inline or drop A-2 from the index.

## 6. Downstream usability — **adequate**

This is a chain-top PRD feeding UX, architecture and story creation, so this dimension carries more weight than usual, and the PRD is built for it: §0 names the downstream consumers, §3 opens with *"FRs, UJs and SMs use them verbatim. A synonym anywhere in this document is a discipline violation"*, and the Glossary is 25 well-drawn entries that mostly are used verbatim. FR IDs FR-1…FR-36 are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates. UJ-1…UJ-4 each have a named protagonist carrying context inline (Éléonore, Emmanuel, Marc, Éléonore) and each is realized by at least one FR — no orphan UJs in the forward direction.

Three problems, in decreasing order.

**Traceability is one-directional.** FRs → UJs works. SMs → FRs is sparse: 17 of 36 FRs are validated by no success metric. Some are defensible (FR-7, FR-11 are hard to measure at product level), but the list includes FR-8 — the *payload schema*, which the PRD itself calls *"The only irreversible decision in the system"* and R-3 rates the highest-consequence risk; FR-13, the only mechanism in the product that can prove an absence; FR-15, the design rule the whole §4.3 argument rests on; FR-19, whose priced statement is UJ-4's climax; FR-21, promoted to a §10 guardrail; FR-30 and FR-33, both written against named v1 defects; and FR-34…FR-36, the i18n block described as *"fatal for a Luxembourg deployment"*. An FR no SM measures is an FR whose regression is invisible, and §9 makes tests the substitute for the engineers who do not exist.

**`OQ-n` identifiers are referenced but never defined.** The PRD cites OQ-3, OQ-4, OQ-5, OQ-6, OQ-7, OQ-8, OQ-9, OQ-10, OQ-11, OQ-12 and OQ-13 (20 references), and the addendum cites OQ-4, OQ-12 and OQ-16. §19 is a plain ordered list `1.`…`17.` — the labels resolve by position only. A downstream agent grepping `OQ-4` finds four references and no definition.

**Glossary drift is present but mostly cosmetic** — with two exceptions that are not. See Mechanical notes; the two that matter for downstream extraction are *document* vs *pièce* on the 100 000 design target, and the three names given to the quantity inside the *confidence bound* sentence.

### Findings
- **high** 17 of 36 FRs are validated by no SM, including FR-8, FR-13, FR-15, FR-19, FR-21, FR-30, FR-33 and FR-34…FR-36 (§7 vs. §4) — the *payload schema*, the absence-proving engine, the *truth status* rule and the whole i18n block have no metric that can fail. *Fix:* add SMs (or extend existing ones) for at least FR-8 (schema completeness/rejection rate at the write boundary), FR-13 (planted-match recall = 100%, zero partial sets labelled exhaustive), FR-15 (zero result sets emitted or exported without a *truth status*) and FR-34…FR-36 (key-set parity, zero hard-coded locales, locale-switched output assertion).
- **medium** `OQ-n` identifiers are cited 23 times across both documents and defined nowhere (§19) — the list is unlabelled; references resolve by position only. *Fix:* prefix each §19 item with its `OQ-n` label.
- **medium** Each §4 feature description carries "Realizes UJ-n" but eight FRs inside those features do not (FR-16, FR-21, FR-25, FR-27, FR-28, FR-29…) — mixed granularity means a story-creation pass extracting per-FR will lose the journey link for the ones missing it. *Fix:* either push the UJ reference down to every FR or state in §0 that feature-level realization is inherited by nested FRs.

## 7. Shape fit — **strong**

The shape is right for the product and the PRD has clearly thought about it. This is a multi-stakeholder B2B product with meaningful, contested UX, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing rather than overhead — and they are used as specification, not as colour: UJ-3's climax (*"He makes twelve corrections in a row and none of them costs him another"*) becomes FR-20 and SM-9 more or less verbatim, and every UJ has a worked edge case that produces a testable consequence downstream. Four UJs for four distinct moments is proportionate; a fifth would have been padding.

The capability/implementation split against `addendum.md` is held better than the norm. The PRD body names no store, no framework, no vendor, no model; the addendum holds Supabase/Vercel/Railway and the acceptable/not-acceptable table, and addendum §6 explicitly lists what it declines to decide. §0's promise — *"It states capabilities and their testable consequences, never technology choices"* — is substantially kept. Four small leaks, none structural: `SYLLOGISME_MIN_SCORE=0` (a v1 environment-variable name, FR-12 and addendum §4); §14's *"Web application"* plus the A-20 browser-surface assumption, which is a client-technology decision made in the body; §9's *"One token set, one colour system, no hard-coded values"*, which is design-system implementation; and §8's corpus table, which names Enron/EDRM and TREC Legal Track and is duplicated near-verbatim in addendum §2 — two copies of the same sourcing decision that will drift.

The brownfield dimension is handled well and unusually: v1 is referenced throughout as a *defect register* rather than as inherited code, each reference is specific (`qdrant.py:37`, `embeddings/factory.py:40`, `api.ts:282`, `SYLLOGISME_MIN_SCORE=0`), and the salvage list in addendum §5 distinguishes what carries over from what does not. The PRD is not over-formalized.

### Findings
- **low** §8's corpus table duplicates addendum §2 near-verbatim (§8 vs. addendum §2) — the same sourcing decision maintained in two places will drift. *Fix:* keep the *strategy* (real content / real mess separation, and the four requirements it imposes) in §8 and let the addendum own the named sources, or state explicitly that §8 is the authoritative copy.
- **low** Minor implementation leaks in the PRD body (FR-12 `SYLLOGISME_MIN_SCORE=0`, §14 "Web application" + A-20, §9 "one token set, one colour system") — small, but §0 sets an absolute standard. *Fix:* move the variable name to the addendum's v1 defect notes; keep §14's platform statement but flag the client-surface choice as an addendum item.

## Mechanical notes

**Glossary drift.**
- ***document* vs *pièce* on the design target.** "100 000 documents" appears in FR-2, FR-6, §6.1, §9, §16, §18 and OQ-6, and is the number every scale-sensitive consequence is asserted against — but *document* is not a §3 term. FR-3 states an email with N attachments yields N+1 *pièces*, so 100 000 documents could mean 100 000 files submitted, 100 000 *pièces* indexed, or something in between, and the gap is a multiple, not a rounding error. Architecture will size against this number. **Fix by restating the design target in *pièces* (or defining *document* in §3).**
- **Three names for the quantity in the *confidence bound*.** The canonical sentence says *"risk of having missed a relevant **document**"* (UJ-2 climax, §3 *Confidence bound*, FR-23); FR-19 says *"risk of having missed a relevant ***pièce***"*; SM-C1 says *"relevant **items***". One of the three sits inside the Glossary definition itself. Pick *pièce* everywhere.
- ***audit trail* for *audit record***. §3's *Audit drawer* entry says *"the proposed **audit trail** entry"* where FR-26 correctly says *"the proposed *audit record* entry"*; §13's heading is "Audit Trail / Decision Provenance". Drift inside the Glossary is the worst place for it.
